\begin{abstract}
We adapt Qwen3.5-4B on public caption--image pairs labeled from figure identities, paper identities, and citation links before gold training on AstroCLIMB. Across three gold-training seeds sharing one public checkpoint, this initialization improves caption--image development macro-F1 by 0.0674 on average over gold-only training, with matched gold schedules but more total compute. Increasing public adaptation from 2,000 to 4,000 updates helps paper-relation classes; 8,000 updates bring no net gain. Answer generation and the reinforcement-learning variants we tested do not beat the classifier. The submitted system combines separate modality classifiers and scores 0.75149 on the Kaggle public leaderboard. Repeated development selection and earlier project exposure confine these findings to the evaluated setting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AstroCLIMB \citep{astroclimb} asks whether two scientific objects come from the same figure, the same paper, citation-related papers, or unrelated papers. Inputs are caption--image (CXI), image--image (IXI), or caption--caption (CXC) pairs; either citation direction counts as related. Its public corpus records figure, paper, and citation structure, providing pair supervision without manual annotation.

We test whether this public supervision helps Qwen3.5-4B \citep{qwen35} before fitting separate modality classifiers with low-rank adaptation (LoRA; \citealp{lora}). Our main evidence is a three-seed matched gold-stage comparison. We also vary adaptation duration and test generative and reinforcement-learning alternatives. The submitted system reaches four-class macro-F1 of \textbf{0.7562033665} on development and \textbf{0.75149} on the Kaggle public leaderboard.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Prior work uses figures and captions for correspondence \citep{lookreadenrich}, medical image--text alignment \citep{subramanian-etal-2020-medicat}, captioning \citep{hsu-etal-2021-scicap-generating}, and multimodal pretraining \citep{sciol}. SPECTER learns document representations from citations \citep{cohan-etal-2020-specter}; we label figure-level pairs. TRACS \citep{grezes-etal-2025-overview} concerns telescope references and categorization, with noncomparable scores.

\paragraph{Public supervision.}
We index all 114 shards of \texttt{adsabs/AstroCLIMB} (94,233 figures) and label CXI pairs directly from figure identity, paper identity, and observed citation edges. From eligible figures we draw 80,000 balanced training pairs and 2,000 validation pairs with disjoint paper components; unrelated pairs mix random-paper, same-journal, and two-hop pairs sharing no direct edge. These labels are not manually verified, and a missing citation edge can produce a noisy negative.

Before generating pairs, we match public papers against every gold split and label-free test content using normalized captions, image-byte and decoded-pixel hashes, and 256-bit dHashes within Hamming distance eight. Dropping every public duplicate-paper component touching a match, 7,500 forbidden papers leave 21,258 eligible figures. The same matches link inherited gold groups through candidate public papers, so we also drop training groups tied to nontraining gold or test content and development groups tied to reserved gold (including exposed fold~0) or test content, removing 450 training and seven development pairs. No matches remain, although incomplete identity coverage may still miss dependencies; test content serves only for exclusion and never carries labels.

Earlier exploration exposed released scoring information and a structural shortcut, used synthetic data overlapping gold content with label-dependent sampling, and relied on misaligned OCR; everything it touched is excluded. The recipe uses no competition test labels, but the exposure cannot be undone.

\paragraph{Selection and reporting.}
Feasibility trials are screened on declared TRAIN-only monitors; public validation never decides when adaptation stops. Whole-task macro-F1 comes from the pooled four-class confusion matrix; because IXI and CXC have no same-figure pairs, we also report active-three-class macro-F1 for them, the four-class logger scoring the absent class as zero.

\section{System}

\paragraph{Task-specific classifiers.}
Each modality classifier adds rank-16 LoRA adapters (alpha 32, effective scaling 2) and a four-class head on the final non-padding representation of a frozen BF16 Qwen3.5-4B base, built with Transformers \citep{wolf-etal-2020-transformers} and PEFT \citep{peft}. The adapters cover the language MLP projections in all 32 layers and the query, key, value, and output projections in the eight full-attention layers, about 21.2M parameters; the vision tower and linear-attention projections stay frozen. Training minimizes cross-entropy without adapter dropout.

CXI prompts hold one image and one caption, IXI prompts Figure~A and Figure~B, and CXC prompts two separated captions. Captions keep their case, truncated at 2,400 characters; images are decoded to RGB from verified original bytes and capped at 262,144 pixels. No prompt contains paper identifiers or retrieved labels; for IXI and CXC the same-figure logit is masked in training and prediction.

\paragraph{Public adaptation and gold specialization.}
Only CXI receives public adaptation: batch size four, two accumulation steps, adapter and head learning rates of $10^{-4}$ and $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, and a 10,000-update warmup/cosine schedule. The submitted model is the checkpoint at update 4,000, after 32,000 pair presentations, without shortening the schedule.

CXI gold training starts from the public adapter and head with a fresh optimizer; IXI and CXC from the pristine base. All three use batch size one, accumulation eight, three epochs, seed~7, and adapter and head learning rates of $3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and $10^{-4}$, giving 837, 627, and 621 updates. We keep the epoch-boundary checkpoint with the highest development macro-F1, earlier on ties; the submitted checkpoints are CXI837, IXI627, and CXC414.

\paragraph{Prediction and reproducibility.}
Each classifier predicts by canonical-class argmax without imposing test class counts; for IXI we average class probabilities over both image orders, fixed before evaluation. The submission uses development-selected models directly, without refitting on all labels.

We hash source, data, model, and prediction artifacts and require a fresh replay before accepting any export. Inference uses batch size one to match training, because batch size two changed 11 of 794 CXI predictions for the public2k-to-gold837 model, with maximum probability difference 0.380. Source, prompts, configurations, and adapter/head weights are public.\ifdefined\CameraReady\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Sekinal/AstroCLIMB}; model downloads are linked there.}\else\footnote{Repository and model links omitted for anonymous review.}\fi Packaged inference is manifest-checked but not GPU-validated, and full retraining from the release is not independently verified. Historical replay probes remain private because they contain training examples.

\section{Results and Analysis}

\paragraph{Matched gold-only controls.}
We compare initialization from public update 4,000 against the pristine base under one CXI gold schedule on one RTX 3090 runtime with seeds 7, 19, and 37. All six runs complete 837 updates, and fresh exports exactly reproduce the selected and final predictions. Total compute is not matched, since the public arm adds its adaptation.

Public initialization improves every seed pair (Table~\ref{tab:matched}; Figure~\ref{fig:results}b), by $0.06738$ on average at the selected checkpoint and $0.06772$ at the final epoch. Both arms select update 558 for seed 19 and 837 otherwise. The public-arm runs share one parent, so seeds vary only the gold stage. The submitted CXI checkpoint, trained on an L40S, scores 0.74839, whereas fresh seed~7 on the RTX 3090 scores 0.75619 from identical parent files, data hashes, and hyperparameters; this cross-runtime difference is unexplained, so fresh controls are compared only within one runtime.

\paragraph{Public adaptation duration.}
A historical comparison fixes the gold schedule and varies public updates. Moving from 2,000 to 4,000 raises CXI development macro-F1 from 0.70851 to 0.74839 (Figure~\ref{fig:results}a), mainly in paper-relation classes: F1 changes by $-0.005$ for same figure, $+0.052$ for same paper, $+0.072$ for related, and $+0.041$ for unrelated, with 81 corrections against 49 regressions on the same 794 pairs. Doubling to 8,000 gives 0.74285; relative to 4,000, same-paper F1 falls by 0.049 while related and unrelated F1 rise by 0.018 and 0.011, with 45 corrections and 47 regressions: a redistribution across classes, not evidence of a capacity ceiling.

Resampling 278 inherited CXI development groups 10,000 times with predictions fixed gives 95\% percentile intervals of [.007,.074] for 2k to 4k, [-.036,.023] for 4k to 8k, and [-.029,.002] for classifier 4k to GSPO512. These intervals reflect only resampling of fixed historical predictions, not selection, training variation, pooled scores, or the three-seed gain; inherited groups may miss dependencies, and an interval spanning zero does not establish equivalence.

\paragraph{Modality specialization.}
Gold training lifts IXI and CXC above frozen-feature baselines. IXI reaches active-class F1 of 0.65536, or 0.67791 with image-order averaging, against 0.54052 for a 75/25 blend of a frozen SigLIP~2 \citep{siglip2} MLP and an OCR-topic logistic model. CXC reaches 0.72756, against 0.67277 for logistic regression on frozen Qwen3-Embedding-8B \citep{qwen3embedding} and BGE-base-en-v1.5 \citep{bgemodel,bgecpack} caption features with TF-IDF and lexical statistics; caption-order averaging gives 0.72721, so we keep original order. With the selected CXI branch the pooled development score is 0.75620; the 8k branch gives 0.75557, so we retain 4k.

\paragraph{Relation-level errors.}
Related-paper pairs remain the weakest class in every modality: pooled F1 is 0.92732 for same figure, 0.70809 for same paper, 0.58582 for related, and 0.80359 for unrelated, and same-paper versus related-paper confusions make up 281 of 549 errors (51.2\%). Our data cannot separate missing visual evidence from noisy supervision.

\paragraph{OCR and auxiliary transfer.}
OCR adds little: correctly aligned PP-OCRv6 \citep{ppocr6} features alone score 0.44142 on CXI, and OCR-conditioned VLM adaptation falls slightly below the pristine gold-only VLM, although a fixed 50/50 combination lifts a superseded whole-system baseline from 0.68987 to 0.69513. Public-to-gold transfer also fails to help a separate frozen-feature IXI classifier, even in a matched-duration follow-up.

\paragraph{Generative training and reinforcement learning.}
Visual-RFT and VLM-R1 \citep{visualrft,vlmr1} motivated visual rewards, RL coverage and reasoning studies \citep{rlvrcoverage,rlvrreasoning} our sampled versus single-answer evaluation, and DeepEyes \citep{deepeyes} fixed-view trials. We apply Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO; \citealp{gspo}) with TRL \citep{trl} to canonical answer generation. Because the classifier-adapted model initially yields only 13 strictly valid answers in 64 completions, we first teach answer tags with 1,024 gold TRAIN pairs and 128 supervised updates.

GSPO uses batch size 16, four completions per context, two uses of each rollout, learning rate $10^{-6}$, group-normalized rewards, no reference-KL term, and asymmetric clipping bounds of 0.0003 and 0.0004; the reward is one only for a correct canonical answer terminated by EOS, and prediction uses the generated answer, not the classifier head.

Neither the 32- nor the 512-update run beats the classifier under the frozen full-development decision rule, though both verifiably update parameters. The 512-update model scores 0.75115, above answer-SFT at 0.74630 and below the classifier at 0.75620; 25 of its 26 corrections are related-paper cases, and all 19 regressions lie elsewhere. A short SFT control matches GSPO32 in parent (answer-SFT128), 64 TRAIN contexts, 32 updates, batch 16, learning rate, and seed~7, but not in tokens, FLOPs, or wall time; without a matched 512-update SFT control, the gain over answer-SFT cannot be credited to reinforcement learning.

Unanimous rewards give zero group-relative advantage, which dynamic sampling \citep{dapo} exploits, so a TRAIN-only trial compares 16 contexts with mixed outcomes among eight samples against 16 class-matched random contexts, each given 32 vLLM-assisted GSPO updates. Mixed selection raises informative groups from 14 to 24 of 32, yet the frozen 256-context TRAIN monitor barely moves (1,874 versus 1,875 correct of 2,048), and neither trial meets the prespecified two-percentage-point screen.

vLLM \citep{vllm} generates rollouts, but evaluation uses native inference because predictions vary across backends; batch shape and BF16 autocast are matched for scoring and reload checks.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

Public initialization improves CXI over gold-only training across three gold-stage seeds, though with one public parent and extra compute; adaptation from 4k to 8k trades performance between classes, generative alternatives do not surpass classification, and all other neural comparisons rest on a single seed.

Repeated development selection and earlier project exposure preclude independent benchmark claims; incomplete citation graphs, imperfect identity recovery, and unknown base-model exposure further limit interpretation; TRAIN monitors exposed to ancestor models serve only as engineering screens.

Future work includes public-stage seed replication, a compute-matched gold-only arm, a long SFT control for GSPO512, and independent evaluation and retraining from the release. Billing does not isolate experiment costs.
