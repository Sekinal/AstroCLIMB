\begin{abstract}
We describe a 4B vision--language system for AstroCLIMB, which classifies relations between scientific figures and captions. A balanced public corpus supplies graph-derived caption--image supervision, followed by separate low-rank adaptations per input modality. The submitted system obtains a Kaggle public score of 0.75149. Under a matched gold adaptation recipe, increasing public adaptation from 2,000 to 4,000 updates improves caption--image development macro-F1 from 0.70851 to 0.74839; 8,000 updates do not improve further. Neither generative supervision nor group sequence policy optimization replacements improve the selected classifier. We document exclusion boundaries, repeated development selection, and inference-context sensitivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AstroCLIMB \citep{astroclimb} asks whether two scientific objects originate from the same figure, the same paper, citation-related papers (an edge in either direction), or unrelated papers. Inputs may be a caption--image pair (CXI), two images (IXI), or two captions (CXC).

We build a content-based system around Qwen3.5-4B \citep{qwen35}, using public graph-derived caption--image supervision followed by task-specific low-rank adaptation (LoRA; \citealp{lora}). The submitted system obtains a four-class macro-F1 of \textbf{0.7562033665} on our development partition and an official Kaggle public score of \textbf{0.75149}. We make no leaderboard-rank claim; our contribution is a system recipe comparing public adaptation duration, OCR, generative supervision, and reinforcement learning, with inference-context checks.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Figure--caption correspondence learning predates this task \citep{lookreadenrich}; MedICaT studies scientific image--text alignment \citep{subramanian-etal-2020-medicat}, and SciCap studies figure captioning \citep{hsu-etal-2021-scicap-generating}. SciOL demonstrates multimodal pretraining on scientific figures and text \citep{sciol}. Citation-derived supervision also underlies SPECTER's document representations \citep{cohan-etal-2020-specter}. Our contribution is thus a task-specific system study, not a new pretraining method. TRACS concerns telescope references and categorization \citep{grezes-etal-2025-overview}; its scores are not comparable.

\section{Data and Evaluation Boundaries}

\paragraph{Gold partitions.}
We release an inherited paper-component assignment, not independently reconstructed, reserving fold~1 for development and folds~2--4 for training. An additional 2,001-pair fold, previously exposed in historical experimentation, is reserved from further tuning and is not an untouched test; we report no new score there.

\paragraph{Public supervision.}
We index all 114 shards of the public \texttt{adsabs/AstroCLIMB} release, containing 94,233 figures, and construct 80,000 balanced CXI training pairs and 2,000 public validation pairs. Labels follow figure/paper identity and observed citation relations; unrelated examples mix random-paper, same-journal, and two-hop pairs without a direct edge. These graph-derived labels are not manually verified: missing edges can produce noisy negatives.

Exclusion checks cover label-free test content and use candidate paper identities, normalized captions, image-byte and decoded-pixel hashes, and 256-bit perceptual hashes at Hamming distance at most eight. Matches propagate through duplicate components and candidate-paper mappings; whole gold training components crossing protected boundaries are removed and public validation components are separated before pair generation. These checks found no retained matches under the audited criteria, but unmatched objects and incomplete metadata prevent a claim of perfect paper-identity recovery. Identifiers and hashes serve only alignment, exclusion, and byte verification, never predictive features. The submitted classifier recipe does not use competition test labels. Earlier exploration exposed released scoring information and a structural shortcut; affected submissions and representations are excluded from this lineage, but the benchmark was not wholly unseen at the project level.

\paragraph{Selection and reporting.}
Main classifier and recipe selection uses development data; feasibility canaries use separately declared TRAIN-only monitors. Reported development scores thus reflect repeated selection, not unbiased estimation or nested cross-validation. Whole-task macro-F1 pools the four-class confusion matrix. IXI and CXC contain no same-figure examples; we report active-three-class macro-F1 separately from the four-class logger that assigns zero to the absent class. Public validation does not determine the adaptation endpoint.

\section{System}

\paragraph{Task-specific classifiers.}
We implement the classifiers with Transformers \citep{wolf-etal-2020-transformers} and PEFT \citep{peft}.
Each branch uses a frozen BF16 Qwen3.5-4B base with rank-16 LoRA (alpha 32, effective scaling 2) and a trainable four-class head over the final non-padding representation. Adapters cover language MLP projections in all 32 layers and query, key, value, and output projections in the eight full-attention layers (about 21.2M parameters); the vision tower and linear-attention projections remain frozen. We use zero adapter dropout and cross-entropy loss.

Prompts contain one image plus caption (CXI), Figure~A and Figure~B (IXI), or two separated captions (CXC). Captions retain original case and are capped at 2,400 characters each. Images are RGB-decoded from verified original bytes and limited to 262,144 pixels per image. Neither paper identifiers nor retrieved labels enter a prompt. IXI and CXC mask the same-figure logit consistently during training and prediction.

\paragraph{Public adaptation and gold specialization.}
Only the submitted CXI branch receives public adaptation. We train on the balanced public corpus with batch size four and two accumulation steps, adapter/head learning rates of $10^{-4}$ and $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, and a 10,000-update warmup/cosine schedule. The submitted parent is the fixed 4,000-update checkpoint, corresponding to 32,000 pair presentations; stopping at this checkpoint does not compress the schedule to 4,000 updates.

Gold adaptation initializes the CXI adapter and head from that parent with a fresh optimizer; IXI and CXC instead initialize independently from the pristine base. All three gold branches use batch size one, accumulation eight, three epochs, seed~7, and adapter/head learning rates of $3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and $10^{-4}$. The resulting schedules contain 837, 627, and 621 updates for CXI, IXI, and CXC respectively. We evaluate at each epoch boundary and select the highest development macro-F1, breaking ties toward the earliest checkpoint. The submitted checkpoints are CXI837, IXI627, and CXC414.

\paragraph{Prediction and reproducibility.}
A prospectively specified arithmetic average of original and reversed image-pair probabilities improves IXI and is retained; the equivalent fixed caption-order average does not improve CXC, so CXC uses its original orientation. We route each pair by modality and take the canonical-class argmax without imposing test class counts. The submission uses these development-selected models, without an all-label final refit.

We preserve source, corpus, checkpoint, processor, and probability-artifact hashes and require fresh replay before accepting exports. Canonical inference uses batch size one: for the public2k-to-gold837 CXI model, batch two changed 11 of 794 predictions (maximum probability difference 0.380), so we retained the training-equivalent contract. Source, configurations, and split artifacts are prepared; public usable checkpoints and independent end-to-end GPU reproduction remain incomplete.

\section{Results and Analysis}

\paragraph{Transfer and modality specialization.}
Figure~\ref{fig:results}a compares public exposure under the same three-epoch gold CXI recipe. Moving from 2,000 to 4,000 public updates improves CXI development macro-F1 by 0.040, with F1 changes of $-0.005$ (same figure), $+0.052$ (same paper), $+0.072$ (related), and $+0.041$ (unrelated). Paired comparison gives 81 corrections against 49 regressions on the same 794 pairs, concentrating the gain in paper-relation decisions. The completed 8,000-update continuation does not improve further (best 0.74285; same-paper F1 $-0.049$, related $+0.018$, unrelated $+0.011$; 45 corrections against 47 regressions), evidence against monotonic benefit from additional synthetic exposure rather than a general scaling law. A separately trained pristine gold-only CXI reference reaches 0.71367 under a different gold schedule and is not a matched control.

Native IXI training reaches active-class F1 of 0.65536, compared with 0.54052 for a frozen SigLIP~2 \citep{siglip2}/OCR feature baseline. The fixed image-order average increases this to 0.67791. Native CXC reaches 0.72756, versus 0.67277 for frozen Qwen3-Embedding-8B \citep{qwen3embedding} and BGE-base-en-v1.5 \citep{bgemodel,bgecpack} caption features. Its order average scores 0.72721 and is rejected. These branches produce the submitted whole-development score of 0.75620; substituting the selected 8k CXI branch yields 0.75557 and is rejected.

\paragraph{Relation-level errors.}
Replaying the saved development predictions exactly recovers the pooled score. Per-class F1 is 0.92732 for same figure, 0.70809 for same paper, 0.58582 for related papers, and 0.80359 for unrelated papers. Related-paper F1 is lowest in every modality. Same-paper/related-paper confusions account for 281 of 549 errors (51.2\%). This post-hoc analysis does not distinguish missing visual evidence from noisy graph supervision.

\paragraph{OCR and auxiliary transfer.}
Correctly aligned PP-OCRv6 \citep{ppocr6} features alone reach only 0.44142 on CXI. An OCR-conditioned VLM adaptation also falls slightly below the pristine gold-only VLM, although a fixed 50/50 combination improves an earlier whole-system development baseline from 0.68987 to 0.69513. Public-to-gold transfer for a separate frozen-feature IXI classifier fails to improve its gold-only counterpart, including a matched-duration follow-up.

\paragraph{Generative training and reinforcement learning.}
Using TRL \citep{trl}, we also study canonical answer generation with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO; \citealp{gspo}). The classifier-adapted model initially produces only 13 strictly valid answers among 64 sampled completions. Answer-tag supervised fine-tuning on 1,024 gold TRAIN pairs, for 128 updates, repairs this interface.

Subsequent GSPO runs use batch size 16, four completions per context, two uses of each rollout, learning rate $10^{-6}$, group-normalized rewards, zero reference-KL weight, reward one only for a correct strict canonical answer terminated by EOS, and asymmetric clipping bounds 0.0003 and 0.0004. These branches predict through the language-model output rather than the classifier head. A 32-update pilot and a 512-update extension both complete genuine gradient and parameter updates. Under the frozen full-development decision rule, neither outperforms the incumbent (Figure~\ref{fig:results}b). The 512-update branch scores 0.75115, above the answer-SFT reference (0.74630) but below the classifier (0.75620). Paired comparison gives 26 corrections (25 related-paper) against 19 regressions (all outside that class), but without a matched 512-update SFT control this does not establish a causal RL gain.



Unanimous group rewards motivate a separate TRAIN-only canary: inspired by dynamic sampling \citep{dapo}, select 16 contexts with mixed outcomes among eight samples and compare against 16 class-matched random contexts. Both receive 32 vLLM-assisted GSPO updates. Mixed selection yields 24 versus 14 informative groups out of 32, but scores on the same frozen 256-context TRAIN monitor are nearly identical: 1,874 versus 1,875 of 2,048. Both miss the prospective two-percentage-point screen, so increased signal does not establish a mining advantage.

The isolated vLLM \citep{vllm} worker accelerates steady generation to about 26.9 examples/s versus 2.1--2.2 native on a fixed 64-example benchmark, but startup costs preclude an end-to-end speedup claim and predictions are backend-dependent. Native inference remains canonical; generation/scoring and fresh-reload checks must match batch shape and BF16 autocast, since successful updates alone do not certify comparable probabilities.

\paragraph{Sources of exploratory ideas.}
Visual-RFT and VLM-R1 \citep{visualrft,vlmr1} motivated testing verifiable visual rewards. Dr.~GRPO \citep{drgrpo} informed our discussion of normalization, but the reported trials retained GSPO. STaR \citep{star} suggested verified explanation bootstrapping, which we did not implement. DeepEyes \citep{deepeyes} motivated a fixed-view feasibility check before any learned crop policy: three-vote accuracy on 64 TRAIN cases fell from 60/64 with repeated full views to 59/64 with a full view and two crops, so learned inspection was not pursued. RL coverage and reasoning work \citep{rlvrcoverage,rlvrreasoning} informed our sampled-answer versus single-answer accuracy distinction, without establishing a general reasoning result.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

Public synthetic adaptation followed by modality-specific specialization is a practical recipe, but longer adaptation and reinforcement learning need not improve the selected classifier. Most neural comparisons use a single seed. Repeated development selection, historically exposed reserved folds, incomplete citation graphs, imperfect identity recovery, unknown base-model pretraining exposure, and ancestor-exposed TRAIN monitors limit statistical and causal claims. The monitor results are engineering screens, not independent test estimates.

Independent evaluation, seed robustness, and improved relation supervision remain future work. Account-wide billing is not a reconciled experiment cost.
