# AI-assistance policy check — 2026-09-21

ACL's publication ethics policy explicitly covers workshops at ACL conferences.
Its generative-assistance section requires full disclosure in acknowledgments,
prohibits AI authorship, and retains human responsibility. Permitted categories
include language assistance, literature search, checked text about existing
ideas, and disclosed ideation developed by authors. This is not blanket approval
of any use merely because it is disclosed.

Source: https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/ACL_Policy_on_Publication_Ethics

The live WASP submission invitation was checked through the public OpenReview
API. Its fields were title, authors, keywords, TLDR, abstract, pdf, email_sharing,
data_release, venue, and venueid. There was no AI-specific declaration field.
The available WASP instructions supplied no additional AI-specific restriction.
This does not establish that no additional organizer guidance exists.

Invitation: https://openreview.net/invitation?id=aclweb.org/AACL-IJCNLP/2026/Workshop/WASP/-/Submission
Workshop: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/WIESP/2026/
OpenReview terms: https://openreview.net/legal/terms

No AI-detector score requirement was found in the checked instructions. This is
not a promise about reviewer behavior. The manuscript acknowledges literature
search, planning, code, orchestration, evidence review, and drafting/revision
across all sections. The sole human author must review and approve the final
claims and citations; automated checks do not replace that responsibility.
