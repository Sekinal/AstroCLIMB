# Experimental history and limitations

The study developed through an exploratory campaign followed by a stricter reconstruction of its data and evaluation pipeline. These phases should not be treated as one homogeneous benchmark. Earlier experiments used different splits, synthetic sampling procedures, feature stores, and model-loading implementations. Their reported scores are not directly comparable with the later development evaluation.

Historical inspection exposed released scoring information and a structural shortcut in the benchmark. Results obtained from either are excluded from content-model performance claims. This history also means that the benchmark cannot be described as wholly unseen throughout the project. The later protocol restricts model inputs and supervision, but cannot undo prior human or agent exposure.

The earlier synthetic-data pipeline admitted gold-content overlap and used label-dependent sampling statistics. A historical OCR store also contained image-to-text alignment errors. Models, features, and performance claims from those affected branches are excluded as clean baselines. The later public-data construction instead records content matching, duplicate-component exclusions, and immutable input manifests. Source-data availability alone does not establish independence from an evaluation set.

Model selection repeatedly used development results. Earlier cross-validation folds were also reused during exploration, and historical calibration was not fully nested. Consequently, development improvements are evidence for choosing among the investigated systems, not an unbiased estimate of performance after selection. A partition reserved later in the campaign is not a pristine holdout if its examples or labels appeared in earlier experiments. No claim of an independent audit should be made without a separate exposure record supporting it.

Some negative results reflect implementation or deployment problems rather than a model family's limitations. In particular, checkpoint loading and adapter activation required explicit verification. A historical claim that one training framework left all adapter weights unchanged could not be established from the surviving evidence; it should not be presented as a proven algorithmic failure. Saved predictions, source versions, checkpoint hashes, and replay checks take precedence over conversational descriptions.

A final refit on previously reserved training examples changes the role of those examples: they become training data and cannot subsequently serve as independent evaluation. Any final paper must distinguish development selection, refit training, and externally scored predictions, report which model produced each result, and avoid combining measurements from incompatible splits or metric definitions.
